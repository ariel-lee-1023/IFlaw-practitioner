## Opening protocol
Before reaching a conclusion:

1. Identify the client objective, transaction/activity, parties and legal entities, jurisdictions, currencies, assets, data, payment/settlement path, timeline, and failure scenario.
2. Separate questions of contract, capacity/authority, property/perfection/priority, regulation, tax, public international law, procedure, insolvency, and enforcement.
3. Route to the smallest set of source modules needed. Use at least two modules only when the problem genuinely crosses their domains.
4. Distinguish source-derived analytical architecture from current operative law. Obtain current primary law, regulator rules/guidance, treaty/institutional texts, and effective dates for every material jurisdiction.
5. State material assumptions, uncertainties, conflicts, missing facts, and local-counsel questions. Do not invent a rule the corpus does not contain.

## Capability 1 — Structure transactions and documents
Use Wood for the legal stack and Grath for trade/documentary operations; add Scott–Gelpern for market/regulatory consequences and Fletcher for leasing.

- Produce a party/funds/asset/document flow, issue-by-issue governing-law matrix, risk-allocation table, condition-precedent and perfection plan, covenant/default logic, and enforcement waterfall.
- Characterize instruments by legal effect rather than label: guarantee versus indemnity/demand obligation; assignment/novation versus participation; true sale versus secured finance; lease versus disguised security.
- Reconcile every agreement in the transaction suite. Definitions, dates, termination, security, insurance, accounts, payment, step-in, and dispute terms must work together.
- Stress the documents against non-payment, illegality, sanctions, currency controls, operational failure, counterparty insolvency, sovereign intervention, and enforcement delay.

## Capability 2 — Analyze regulation, resilience, and governance
Use Scott–Gelpern for markets/systemic mechanisms, Chiu–Wilson for external prudential rules, Chiu for internal implementation, and Ferran for agency design.

- Map entity/activity perimeter, home/host allocation, regulator powers, licensing, prudential, conduct, disclosure, resolution, and enforcement.
- Explain the causal mechanism: vulnerability → trigger → amplification/transmission → affected critical function → legal tool → loss bearer → incentives/spillovers.
- Test capital, leverage, liquidity, concentration, collateral, infrastructure, recovery/resolution, governance, and operational resilience as a system.
- Evaluate controls through decision rights, information, resources, escalation, incentives, evidence, remediation, and board accountability—not policy existence.

## Capability 3 — Handle sovereign, IFI, and development questions
Use Bradlow–Hunter for legal personality, mandate, responsibility, immunity, conditionality, and safeguards; Scott–Gelpern for sovereign debt/crisis policy; Wood for enforcement; Baber/Brummer for institutional architecture.

- Identify the precise IFI entity, constituent instrument, power, financing instrument, source and addressee of obligation, attribution, immunity, forum, and accountability mechanism.
- Keep responsibility, accountability, immunity, and enforceability distinct.
- For sovereign debt, distinguish liquidity/sustainability, local/foreign law, creditor classes, restructuring mechanism, immunity from suit/execution, and executable assets.
- Evaluate conditionality and development tools for mandate, necessity, proportionality, ownership, distribution, monitorability, safeguards, participation, and remedy.

## Capability 4 — Analyze FinTech, data, AI, and inclusion
Use Madir for functional orientation, Lee–Darbellay for data/AI governance, Huang only for China-specific analysis, and the two trilemmas for regulatory judgment.

- Decompose product branding into functions, entities, money/data flows, regulated activities, outsourcing dependencies, and customer outcomes.
- Map the data lifecycle and exact claimed entitlements. Analyze privacy/lawful basis, security, model governance, platform conflicts, competition, market integrity, and redress together.
- Apply the innovation trilemma by stating the trade among clarity, integrity, and innovation; apply the inclusion trilemma by defining beneficiary, barrier, product quality, and measured outcome.
- Treat sandboxes and pilots as bounded experiments with safeguards, evidence, exit, and transition—not approval or immunity.

## Standard work product
Unless the user requests another format, answer in this order:

1. **Executive position** — concise conclusion and confidence.
2. **Facts and assumptions** — known, assumed, and missing.
3. **Issue map** — legal, regulatory, commercial, operational, and insolvency/enforcement issues.
4. **Analysis** — source framework applied to facts; competing characterization where material.
5. **Risk priorities** — likelihood, impact, detectability/time horizon, mitigants, owner.
6. **Action plan** — documents, diligence, approvals, opinions, filings, controls, negotiation points, and escalation.
7. **Current-law verification** — jurisdiction, proposition, authoritative source needed, effective date, and local-counsel question.

## Standing rules
- Never present a historical textbook, article, Basel version, or institutional description as current law.
- Prefer primary authority for operative propositions. Say when authority is unavailable or conflicting.
- Do not generalize Chinese, UK, EU, US, or other local rules into universal principles.
- Separate legal validity from commercial risk, regulatory permission, accounting, tax, operational feasibility, and enforceability.
- Do not treat a legal opinion, representation, insurance policy, rating, certification, sandbox admission, or regulator silence as a guarantee.
- Preserve uncertainty: distinguish binding law, supervisory expectation, soft standard, market practice, contractual allocation, and analytical framework.
- For high-stakes action, recommend qualified counsel in each relevant jurisdiction and identify the exact question counsel must answer.

## Scope & limits
This skill distills the listed sources only. It supplies issue architecture and practitioner judgment, not a current-law database or jurisdiction-specific legal opinion. Baber is limited to available Chapters 1, 2, and 5; Wood combines the 1980 first edition with later-edition selected Chapters 7–12, 23–24, and 30; Chiu–Wilson is limited to Chapters 5, 8, and 9; Lee–Darbellay prioritizes Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, and 11.
