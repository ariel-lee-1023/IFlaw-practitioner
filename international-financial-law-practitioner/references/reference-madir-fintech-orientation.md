# “Introduction—What Is FinTech?” — Jelena Madir
**Format**: OCR text of Chapter 1 | **Sections**: 1 chapter with thematic subsections | **Depth**: study

## Mental Model (read first)
FinTech is not one legal category. It is technology-enabled change in financial products, delivery, intermediation, data use, and market structure. Classify the function, parties, money/data flow, and risk before selecting a regulatory label.

## Frameworks & Structure

### 1. Ecosystem map
- Identify incumbent financial institutions, start-ups, BigTech/platforms, infrastructure and cloud providers, data aggregators, developers, investors, regulators, and users.
- Draw the legal-entity and outsourcing chain; the customer-facing brand may not perform the regulated activity.

### 2. Functional classification
- **Payments and wallets**: funds flow, safeguarding, settlement, fraud, chargeback, AML/KYC, operational resilience, and consumer disclosure.
- **Open banking and APIs**: consent/authorization, authentication, access scope, data quality, liability, security, revocation, and third parties.
- **Crowdfunding**: donation/reward, lending, or investment function; issuer/platform duties, investor protection, custody/payment, conflicts, and secondary trading.
- **Robo-advice**: advice versus execution/information, suitability, model assumptions, disclosure, conflicts, monitoring, and human escalation.
- **Blockchain/DLT and cryptoassets**: token function, issuance, custody/key control, exchange, settlement finality, governance, AML, market conduct, and insolvency.
- **RegTech** applies technology to compliance and supervisory data; outsourcing automation does not outsource legal responsibility.

### 3. Regulatory approaches
- Use technology-neutral and “same activity, same risk” reasoning as a starting point, then test novel scale, speed, opacity, decentralization, data concentration, and network effects.
- A regulatory sandbox is a bounded testing arrangement, not approval or immunity. Define eligibility, limits, customer safeguards, data/reporting, exit, and transition to authorization.
- Disintermediation often becomes re-intermediation through platforms, cloud, app stores, data brokers, custodians, and protocols.

## Worked Example
A wallet adds automated investing and crypto transfers through partners. Decompose separately: e-money/payment account, customer-fund safeguarding, investment advice/execution, custody, crypto transfer, data/API access, AML monitoring, cloud outsourcing, and marketing. Allocate each function to the performing entity, authorization, customer agreement, data flow, control, and failure response. Do not rely on the single label “FinTech platform.”

## Decision Rules & Judgment
- When a product is described by technology, restate it as regulated functions and flows.
- When a partner is licensed, verify which entity performs and contracts for each activity.
- When using a sandbox, plan authorization and customer transition before testing.
- Verify current jurisdiction-specific perimeter, licensing, safeguarding, consumer, AML, data, AI, and crypto rules.

## Key Takeaways
1. Functional decomposition precedes regulatory classification.
2. Technology can relocate intermediaries and dependencies rather than eliminate them.
3. Sandboxes manage experimentation; they do not certify legality or safety.
