# Leasing in Development — Matthew Fletcher, Rachel Freeman, Murat Sultanov, and Umed Temirbek
**Format**: pdf | **Pages**: 78 | **Sections**: 3 chapters | **Depth**: study

## Mental Model (read first)
Leasing can expand productive-asset access where collateral, credit information, and enforcement systems constrain ordinary lending. A viable leasing market depends on the whole ecosystem: commercial law, repossession, registration, prudential treatment, accounting, tax, supervision, funding, professional capacity, and customer understanding.

## Frameworks & Structure

### 1. Development rationale and market diagnosis
- Leasing separates use from ownership and may rely on the leased asset and cash generation rather than conventional real-estate collateral.
- Diagnose demand by firm segment, asset type, ticket size, tenor, currency, supplier channel, informality, and credit constraints.
- Assess supply: lessors, bank/non-bank status, funding sources, foreign exchange, pricing, residual-value capacity, servicing, and secondary markets.
- **Anti-pattern — transplanting a product**: importing documentation or tax assumptions without testing local title, repossession, registration, and accounting.

### 2. Legal framework
- Define finance lease and distinguish operating lease, hire-purchase, secured loan, conditional sale, and rental under local law.
- Allocate title, possession, maintenance, insurance, taxes, loss/damage, warranties, acceptance, assignment, default, termination, repossession, sale, shortfall, and residual value.
- Effective enforcement needs clear lessor ownership, priority against lessee creditors, accessible asset registration where appropriate, and timely repossession subject to due process.
- Insolvency law must clarify treatment of leased assets and disguised-security/recharacterization risk.

### 3. Regulation and supervision
- Decide whether lessors require licensing/registration, prudential standards, governance, reporting, conduct rules, AML/KYC, and complaints based on funding model and risk.
- Avoid regulating asset-funded non-deposit lessors exactly like banks without a risk rationale; avoid leaving consumer/customer and systemic risks unaddressed.
- Collect market data that distinguishes genuine leases from loans or rentals.

### 4. Accounting and taxation
- Accounting classification affects balance sheets, income recognition, provisioning, and ratios. Tax rules affect depreciation, VAT/sales tax, withholding, deductions, and transfer/import costs.
- Seek neutrality across economically similar financing where possible; tax preferences can jump-start a market but also create avoidance or product distortion.
- Model after-tax cash flows for lessor and lessee, including asset disposal and early termination.

### 5. Market-development sequence
- Review laws and institutions → consult market participants → design targeted reforms → develop standard documents and registries → train lessors, judiciary, and regulators → support pilot transactions → monitor performance and revise.
- Pair legal reform with operational capacity; a sound statute without funding, skills, enforcement, or asset markets will not create leasing.

## Worked Example
A development institution supports agricultural-equipment leasing for SMEs. Diagnose seasonal cash flows, equipment suppliers, import/currency risk, maintenance network, asset identification/registration, insurance, repossession, resale value, and farmer protection. Model accounting and tax for both parties, determine licensing and reporting, draft payment schedules matching harvest cycles, and pilot with performance data. Measure productive use, repayment, recovery time, customer outcomes, and whether access is additional rather than replacing cheaper credit.

## Decision Rules & Judgment
- When leasing is proposed as collateral-light finance, verify title and repossession in practice.
- When legal form differs from economic substance, test recharacterization, accounting, tax, and insolvency.
- When granting incentives, define the market failure, duration, eligibility, and anti-abuse controls.
- When using this development guide, verify current local law and accounting/tax standards.

## Key Takeaways
1. Leasing-market development is an ecosystem project, not a template contract.
2. Ownership, priority, repossession, tax, and accounting determine commercial viability.
3. Regulation should match funding, conduct, and systemic risks.
