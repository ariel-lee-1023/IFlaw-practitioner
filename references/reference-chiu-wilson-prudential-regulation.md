## Mental Model (read first)
External prudential regulation specifies the resilience outcomes and supervisory architecture that a bank’s internal governance must implement. Read capital, liquidity, leverage, large exposures, systemic-bank buffers, stress testing, supervision, and resolution together: optimizing one metric can move risk into another.

## Frameworks & Structure

### 1. International banking supervision
- International standards are commonly developed through bodies such as the Basel Committee and implemented through domestic or regional law. Distinguish non-binding standard, binding implementing rule, supervisory guidance, and firm-specific requirement.
- Cross-border banking requires home/host allocation, consolidated supervision, supervisory colleges, information sharing, equivalence or cooperation, and crisis-management coordination.
- **Basel Core Principles for Effective Banking Supervision** provide minimum supervisory standards; an assessment of compliance is not the same as direct legal enforceability.
- Map authorization, ongoing supervision, governance, risk management, reporting, corrective powers, recovery/resolution, and deposit protection.

### 2. Capital adequacy
- Capital requirements aim to absorb unexpected loss and protect continuity, but outputs depend on capital quality, exposure measure, risk weights, internal/standardized models, deductions, and buffers.
- Organize the Basel framework through **Pillar 1** minimum quantitative requirements, **Pillar 2** supervisory review and firm assessment, and **Pillar 3** market discipline/disclosure.
- Separate going-concern capital from gone-concern loss-absorbing capacity; verify current definitions and transitional rules.
- Examine credit, market, operational, counterparty, concentration, and model risk. Capital ratios can rise by increasing genuine loss absorption or by reducing measured risk; these are not equivalent.

### 3. Liquidity and funding
- The **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)** addresses short-term stress survival using high-quality liquid assets against modeled net outflows.
- The **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)** addresses structural funding over a longer horizon.
- Test asset monetizability, encumbrance, currency/location mismatch, intraday liquidity, collateral calls, deposit behavior, wholesale rollover, central-bank access, and contingency funding.
- **Anti-pattern — buffer double counting**: assuming the same asset is freely available across legal entities, currencies, payment needs, and stress scenarios.

### 4. Leverage, large exposures, and systemically important banks
- A leverage ratio supplies a non-risk-weighted backstop when risk measurements understate exposure.
- Large-exposure limits constrain single-name or connected-counterparty concentration; identify control and economic interdependence, not merely formal borrower names.
- G-SIB/D-SIB frameworks add systemic buffers and enhanced supervision based on size, interconnectedness, substitutability, complexity, and cross-border activity.
- Loss-absorbing capacity and resolution planning should be examined by issuing entity, ranking, trigger, governing law, and recognition across borders.

### 5. Stress testing
- Stress tests convert scenarios into capital/liquidity effects under specified balance-sheet, behavioral, and management-action assumptions.
- Review scenario design, reverse stress, model validation, data lineage, legal-entity granularity, second-order effects, governance, challenge, disclosure, and remediation.
- Use stress results to change limits, buffers, funding plans, recovery options, and strategy; a test with no decision consequence is weak assurance.

## Worked Example
For a bank expanding cross-border wholesale lending, map exposures by obligor group, country, currency, booking entity, maturity, collateral, and funding source. Test risk-weighted capital and leverage constraints, concentration limits, LCR/NSFR and intraday needs, entity-level transfer restrictions, and a combined downgrade–deposit outflow–margin-call scenario. Then connect external requirements to board appetite, limits, treasury, risk, compliance, audit, recovery planning, and regulatory reporting.

## Decision Rules & Judgment
- When quoting a Basel standard, identify its domestic implementation and applicable date.
- When a group ratio looks comfortable, test entity, currency, and transferability constraints.
- When risk weights fall, compare leverage and concentration measures before concluding resilience improved.
- When a stress test passes, inspect assumptions and management actions before relying on the result.

## Key Takeaways
1. International standards become operative through jurisdiction-specific implementation and supervision.
2. Capital, leverage, liquidity, concentration, and resolution constraints are complementary.
3. Consolidated strength cannot automatically be moved to the entity or currency where stress occurs.
4. Prudential rules must be translated into internal governance and evidence.
